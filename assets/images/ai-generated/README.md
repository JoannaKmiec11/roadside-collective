## The founder portrait, added 12 November 2026

This one needs saying plainly because it is the highest risk image on the site.

It is **AI generated and it is not Joanna.** It is in place for the course submission on 13 November 2026, with the disclosure line visible next to it, and **a real photograph replaces it before the site is promoted to anybody.**

Why it matters more than the pub images: that section exists to answer "will I be the only one there who does not know anyone", and the answer is meant to be "no, Joanna will be at the table by the door, and here she is". A face that is not hers is a false answer to the question the page was built to answer. Somebody turns up on 13 December and meets a different person.

So it ships **only** with:

1. The disclosure line next to it, not in a footer. "Placeholder portrait, AI generated. A real photograph replaces it before launch."
2. Alt text saying the same thing.
3. A hard stop: it does not survive the site going public, the flyer, Instagram, or Eventbrite's organiser profile.

Cropped square from a 768 x 1365 original. The crop also removes the generator's sparkle badge from the bottom corner, which is a detail worth noticing: **the badge was the only visible sign it was generated, and cropping removed it.** That is exactly why the written disclosure has to carry the weight instead.

These are usable. They are kept apart from the rest of `assets/images/` so that nobody has to guess which images are generated, because the answer changes what has to go next to them.

## The hero image, added 12 November 2026

`pub-tv-centred-` is the clean version of the pub scene, with no cream card baked into it. The Canva composite with the card, `landing-hero-1216x684.png`, is **not** the one to use on the site, because the site draws its own text.

It is the "TV centred" variant rather than `pub-watching`, because the screen sits where the oval mirror used to be, so every gaze in the room converges on it instead of competing with a mirror.

**Why this placement is acceptable where the earlier one was not.** The hero copy beside it says "We meet in Brighton pubs" and carries no date and no venue. The image is setting a scene, not claiming to be a specific room on a specific Sunday. The version that was rejected sat directly above a date, a venue and a signup button, where it would have been answering "what will the room be like on the night".

It still ships with the disclosure line, and it still comes down when there is a photograph of the real pub.

## Before any of these is published

1. **Carry the AI disclosure line.** It is a standing Brand Bible rule and its wording is still open. A draft is in BRAND.md.
2. **Write alt text**, which is a standing rule with no exceptions. Describe the image, and say it is an illustration rather than a photograph of the venue.
3. **Do not caption it as the venue.** The pub is not booked. An image that reads as a photograph of the room, next to a date and a street, is a claim about a real place.

Google applies an invisible SynthID watermark to images generated with its own models, so these are detectable as generated whether or not we say so. That is an argument for disclosing rather than a substitute for it.

## Where the line is

The site and the posters exist to tell a stranger what the room will be like on the night, so they can decide whether to walk into a pub alone. That makes the honest use of these images narrow.

**Fine:** an illustrative header on an explainer article about cyclo-cross, which is what `pub-watching` is doing now. It sets a mood for a piece about a sport. It is not pretending to be anywhere.

**Not fine:** the hero of the landing page, directly above a date, a venue and a signup button. In that position the image is answering "what will the room be like", and a generated answer to that question is a misleading one. The content plan already says this about the Thursday post: a real photograph of the real room, taken on a phone when the booking is confirmed, beats anything generated.

**So `landing-hero-1216x684.png` is deliberately unused.** The landing page is specified with no hero image at all until there is a photograph of the actual pub. That is a decision, not an oversight.
